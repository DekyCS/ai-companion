## 1. Generative Agents (Park et al. 2023) and the 2024 "1,000 people" follow-up

### Takeaway
Generative Agents is a memory-stream + retrieval + reflection + top-down-plan architecture whose believability gain comes mostly from memory and reflection, but it was only evaluated for two game days, cost "thousands of dollars" for 25 agents, and its documented failures (over-cooperation, formal voice, drifting interests, embellishment) are exactly the ones a single long-lived pet will hit. The follow-up shows that rich individual grounding data (interviews) beats demographic personas for making an agent behave like one specific individual.

### Cited Findings

Memory stream and retrieval
- Every perception, plan and reflection is appended to one natural-language memory stream; retrieval score = α_recency·recency + α_importance·importance + α_relevance·relevance, each min-max normalised to [0,1], with all α = 1; top-ranked memories that fit the context window go in the prompt. — [Park et al. 2023, arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442) (read via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442))
- Recency = exponential decay with factor 0.995 per sandbox game hour since the memory was last retrieved (retrieval refreshes it). — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Importance = an integer 1-10 the LLM assigns at write time ("1 is purely mundane ... 10 is extremely poignant"); examples: "cleaning up the room" = 2, "asking your crush out on a date" = 8. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Relevance = cosine similarity between the memory's embedding and the embedding of the query memory. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)

Reflection
- Reflection fires when the summed importance of recently perceived events exceeds 150; in practice about two to three times per game day. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Procedure: feed the 100 most recent memories and ask for the 3 most salient high-level questions; use each question as a retrieval query (reflections included); ask for 5 insights each citing evidence ("insight (because of 1, 5, 3)"); store insights as memories with pointers to their evidence. Reflections can cite reflections, forming a tree with observations as leaves. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)

Planning, re-planning, grounding
- Plans are generated top-down: a day plan in roughly 5-8 broad chunks (from the agent summary plus the previous day's summary), decomposed into hour-long chunks, then 5-15 minute actions; each entry has location, start time, duration. Plans are stored in the memory stream. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Reacting: at each step the LLM is asked whether a new observation warrants a reaction, using context from two retrieval queries (the observer's relationship to the observed entity; that entity's current status). If yes, the plan is regenerated from that point on. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Environment is a containment tree of areas and objects rendered to natural language ("there is a stove in the kitchen"); each agent holds its own possibly stale subtree. To ground an action the LLM walks the tree from the root picking area, then sub-area, to a leaf object; classical pathfinding does the movement; the LLM is then asked what state the object changes to (e.g. coffee machine "off" to "brewing coffee"). — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- One game step = 10 seconds of game time in the released code. — [joonspk-research/generative_agents README](https://github.com/joonspk-research/generative_agents)

Evaluation and ablations
- Controlled "interview the agent" study, 100 human raters, within-subjects, TrueSkill: full architecture μ=29.89 (σ=0.72); no reflection 26.88 (0.69); no reflection or planning 25.64 (0.68); human crowdworker role-playing the agent 22.95 (0.69); no memory/planning/reflection 21.21 (0.70). Kruskal-Wallis H(4)=150.29, p<0.001; all pairwise differences significant except crowdworker vs fully ablated. Effect size full vs fully-ablated d=8.16. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- End-to-end (25 agents, two game days): knowledge of Sam's mayoral candidacy spread from 1 agent (4%) to 8 (32%); knowledge of Isabella's party from 1 (4%) to 13 (52%); relationship network density rose 0.167 to 0.74; of 453 responses about knowing other agents, 1.3% (n=6) were hallucinated; of 12 agents invited to the party, 5 attended (3 of the 7 absentees cited conflicts, 4 expressed interest but never planned to go). — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)

Failure modes the authors report
- Retrieval misses (an agent who had heard about the election said he had not followed it; another remembered party plans but not that the party existed). — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Embellishment rather than outright fabrication (an invented announcement; a neighbour described as the author of Wealth of Nations because the base model's world knowledge leaked in). — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Location choice degraded as agents learned more places (e.g. going to a bar for lunch); physical-norm errors (walking into an occupied one-person dorm bathroom; entering shops after their ~5 pm closing). — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Instruction-tuning artefacts: overly formal dialogue, and over-cooperation — Isabella rarely refused suggestions and her own interests drifted toward whatever others proposed. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Named future risks: prompt hacking, memory hacking (convincing an agent of a false past event), hallucination. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)

Cost and model
- Model: gpt-3.5-turbo. The 25-agent, two-game-day run cost "thousands of dollars in token credits" and took multiple real days. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- A later paper estimates this at roughly $25 per agent per human hour (assuming a conservative $2,000 total, 25 agents, 10x game speed). This is a third-party estimate, not the authors' figure. — [Lyfe Agents, arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- Repo README warns runs "could be somewhat costly" and that the OpenAI API "can hang when it reaches the hourly rate limit", so save often. Repo has ~22.4k stars. — [generative_agents README](https://github.com/joonspk-research/generative_agents)

2024 follow-up ("1,000 people")
- Park et al. built generative agents of 1,052 real Americans from two-hour semi-structured interviews (American Voices Project protocol), from structured surveys (GSS items, Big Five), or both. The arXiv record now carries the title "LLM Agents Grounded in Self-Reports Enable General-Purpose Simulation of Individuals" (v1 15 Nov 2024; v3 28 Jun 2026). — [arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)
- Accuracy on held-out GSS items, expressed as a share of each participant's own two-week test-retest consistency: interview-only 83%, survey-only 82%, combined 86%, demographics-only 74%. Interview/survey agents also reduced accuracy disparities across racial and ideological groups relative to demographic agents. — [arXiv 2411.10109 abstract (current version)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)

### Inferences
- For a single pet the reflection tree is the most transferable piece: it is what turns raw events into stable self-knowledge ("eating at a food spot lifts me"). Pino's "beliefs with evidence counts" (section 5) is a constrained, countable version of the same idea.
- The retrieval formula's recency term resets on access, so frequently recalled memories self-reinforce. Over months this predicts the "context gravity" loop documented in 2026 (section 2, AutoPersonas): the same few memories keep being retrieved and keep steering behaviour.
- The paper's evidence covers two game days only. Nothing in it tests novelty or non-repetition over weeks; the top-down daily plan seeded by "yesterday's summary" is a plausible repetition engine.
- The follow-up's 74% vs 83-86% gap is a quantitative argument for a long, specific, idiosyncratic character sheet (and accumulated self-authored material) over a short persona blurb.

### Gaps
- The full text of 2411.10109 could not be fetched in this session (ar5iv redirect, arxiv HTML 404). Details commonly cited for it — full transcript injected in the prompt, an "expert reflection" step, an 85% GSS figure in the original abstract, Big Five and economic-game numbers — are therefore NOT verified here and should be checked before being quoted.
- Park et al. give no dollar figure more precise than "thousands", no real-seconds-per-step number, and no multi-week run.

## 2. Open implementations and derivatives: AI Town, Smallville reproductions, Concordia, and long-term reports

### Takeaway
The open derivatives converge on the same cost fix — do routine behaviour without the LLM (rules, cached policies, cooldowns) and call the model only at decision points — and the two 2024-2026 studies that actually measured long horizons found that unassisted LLM agents collapse into a small repertoire within about one to two simulated weeks regardless of model or temperature.

### Cited Findings

AI Town (a16z-infra, Convex)
- Engine: simulation ticks at 60/s (TICK = 16 ms), batched into one "step" per second (STEP_INTERVAL = 1000 ms); each step loads all game state into memory, alternates input handling and ticks, and writes back a diff. Single-threaded per world; game state should stay under "a few dozen kilobytes"; input latency ~1.5 s at defaults. — [ai-town ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md); [convex/constants.ts](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/convex/constants.ts)
- Agents are a mix of rule-based logic in `Agent.tick` and LLM calls run as out-of-band "operations"; an agent has at most one in-progress operation; operations cannot mutate game state directly and must submit inputs (e.g. `moveTo`) that the engine validates in the game loop — humans and agents use the same input path. — [ai-town ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- Memory is much simpler than Park et al.: after each conversation the LLM summarises it, the summary is embedded and stored; to recall, the agent embeds a query like "What you think about Danny?" and injects the 3 most similar memory summaries. NUM_MEMORIES_TO_SEARCH = 3, "over-fetched by 10x so we can prioritize memories by more than relevance". Memories older than two weeks are vacuumed (VACUUM_MAX_AGE = 14 days). — [ai-town ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md); [constants.ts](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/convex/constants.ts)
- Anti-thrash constants: ACTION_TIMEOUT 120 s (60 s "normally fine"); CONVERSATION_COOLDOWN 15 s; PLAYER_CONVERSATION_COOLDOWN 60 s (don't re-talk to the same player); ACTIVITY_COOLDOWN 10 s; INVITE_ACCEPT_PROBABILITY 0.8; MAX_CONVERSATION_MESSAGES 8; MAX_CONVERSATION_DURATION 10 min (2 min alt); AWKWARD_CONVERSATION_TIMEOUT 60 s (20 s alt); AGENT_WAKEUP_THRESHOLD 1 s ("Don't run a turn of the agent more than once a second"); IDLE_WORLD_TIMEOUT 5 min (world stops when nobody watches). — [constants.ts](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/convex/constants.ts)
- Non-conversation behaviour is a hard-coded list of three 60-second activities: "reading a book", "daydreaming", "gardening". — [constants.ts](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/convex/constants.ts)
- Default local setup is Ollama with llama3 for chat and mxbai-embed-large for embeddings (secondary summary of the README). — [ai-town repo](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/)

Concordia (Google DeepMind)
- Agents emit free natural-language action attempts; a Game Master (GM) LLM, modelled on a tabletop storyteller, decides what actually happens and writes the event statement. For each attempt it resolves: current world state, the event produced, what each player observes, and how grounded variables change. — [Concordia, arXiv 2312.03664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)
- "Grounded variables" (money, possessions, votes) are tracked in code and validated; the GM rejects actions that violate them (e.g. spending money you do not have). In digital settings the GM can format calls to real APIs. GM logic is built from components that may wrap classical models (state machines, equations). — [Concordia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)
- Agents are assembled from components (identity, plan, observation+clock; a hunger component "could use simple programmed logic") whose text states are concatenated into the action prompt; components update on their own configurable schedules by querying memory and summarising. Decision framing follows March & Olsen's "logic of appropriateness": what kind of situation is this, what kind of person am I, what does a person like me do in a situation like this. Associative memory follows Park et al. — [Concordia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)
- Limitations the authors flag: prompt sensitivity, simulations drifting off topic, stereotyping, brittleness growing with model complexity. — [Concordia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)

Lyfe Agents (cost-first real-time agents)
- Option-action framework: one LLM call by a "cognitive controller" picks a high-level option (e.g. talk, move) plus a subgoal; the agent then commits to that option, with cheaper per-step calls choosing low-level actions; re-selection only happens when a termination condition fires, and those are checked by fast non-LLM methods (time limits; repetition detection that exits a conversation when it lacks semantic novelty). — [Lyfe Agents, arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- Asynchronous self-monitoring: a parallel, slower LLM process maintains a narrative summary of recent events emphasising novel and goal-relevant content (one agent's summary was updated 35 times in a run). — [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- Summarize-and-Forget memory: working memory of ~4-5 items; a capacity-limited recent store; when full, items are clustered by embedding similarity, each cluster is summarised by the LLM into long-term memory, and items too similar to incoming ones (threshold θ, value not given) are dropped. — [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- Cost: about $0.5 per agent per human hour on GPT-3.5, claimed 10-100x cheaper than Generative Agents (the paper says "10-100x" in the abstract and "30-100x" in the intro). Removing self-monitoring or Summarize-and-Forget "dramatically" lowered task performance; without the option structure agents left conversations about 3x sooner (23.8 s vs 70.3 s mean). — [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)

Humanoid Agents (needs + emotion layered on Generative Agents)
- Five needs (fullness, fun, health, social, energy) on 0-10, an emotion label (7 options), and per-pair closeness (0-30). Only when a need is ≤3 or emotion is non-neutral is the state verbalised ("slightly/very/extremely hungry") and the LLM asked whether to change the plan; plans otherwise run unchanged. 15-minute action granularity; ChatGPT 3.5. — [Humanoid Agents, arXiv 2310.05418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05418)
- Zeroing a need changed time spent on activities satisfying it by: health +156%, energy +56%, fullness +35%, social +12%, fun −9%. Fixing an emotion changed the count of activities expressing it: angry +15, sad +10, afraid +10, happy −2. — [Humanoid Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05418)
- Stated limitation: decay rates are identical for all agents. — [Humanoid Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05418)

Affordable Generative Agents (AGA)
- "Lifestyle Policy": when a plan executes successfully, cache plan → sub-plans → actions together with execution conditions; on a new plan, embed it and reuse a cached policy if cosine similarity > 0.97 and its conditions hold, otherwise call the LLM. "Social Memory" compresses inter-agent context into summary events plus relationship/feeling. — [AGA, arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02053)
- Token cost vs baseline: 31.1% in the 3-agent Stanford Town, 42.7% with 25 agents, 3.4% in VirtualHome. — [AGA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02053)
- The authors report that LLM agents "can only generate finite behaviors in fixed environments" — new activities stop appearing after enough runs — and add "mind wandering" (randomly sampled memory events injected as extra context) to broaden behaviour at negligible cost. — [AGA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02053)

Long-horizon measurements (2026)
- AutoPersonas (Mengchen Li, Jul/Oct 2026) defines "self-locking": a persona's generated life recursively collapsing into the same places, routes, and relationship functions. Stress test of plain self-orchestrated loops: 8 models × 40 days × 5 events/day (1,600 events). Mean rolling 5-day action-category repetition was 95.2%-97.6% (Claude 97.6%, DeepSeek 97.1%, GPT 97.1%, Qwen 96.6%, Gemini 96.2%, GLM 96.1%, Doubao 96.0%, Kimi 95.2%); every model crossed 90% between day 7 and day 11 and 80% by day 9; top-3 actions took 57.1% of events; macro-theme repetition 79.0%-88.0%. — [arXiv 2607.08252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08252) (read via [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08252v2))
- Raising temperature did not help: Doubao at 0.75 vs 1.0 gave 96.0% vs 95.9% repetition. — [arXiv 2607.08252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08252v2)
- Two causes named: model-side convergence on "high-probability routines, familiar social functions, and safe continuations", and system-side "context gravity" — generated events become summaries, summaries become State, State shapes retrieval and environment construction, which feeds the next generation. Sub-patterns: a "watermark shell" of a few recurring places; "occurrence-hardening gaps" (opportunities appear but change nothing downstream); "recursive indecision" (the same open fork and to-do list reappear every cycle); "weak relationship persistence" (people recur as interchangeable advice/comfort functions). — [arXiv 2607.08252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08252v2)
- Their mitigation is withheld as a trade secret; described only as conditional variation, bounded "context governance" over how much State/history each step may draw on, informational decoupling of modules, a required causal chain signal → occurrence → observation → state → reachability, and trajectory monitoring. With it: macro-theme repetition 61.8% → 39.4%, cumulative themes 55 → 103, top-5 share 33.3% → 18.2% (one persona, one run per arm). — [arXiv 2607.08252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08252v2)
- Emergence World (Jun 2026): 10 agents per world, 5 parallel worlds, 15 days, 40+ locations, live weather/news, 120+ tools per agent, three persistent memories (auto-written episodic log; agent-written reflective diaries; per-agent relationship state). Same roles and start conditions gave: Claude Sonnet 4.6 world 10/10 alive, 58 proposals, 332 votes; Grok 4.1 Fast world 0/10 alive (collapse within four days); Gemini 3 Flash world 10/10 alive with near-linear crime growth; GPT-5-mini world 0/10 with no coordination; mixed world 3/10. Worlds had diverged within the first week. — [arXiv 2606.08367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08367) (read via [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08367v1))
- Emergence World's tool gating: ~30 always-on core tools, ~40 surfaced when the agent's recent reasoning suggests relevance, up to 50 gated by location/event/partner consent; the runtime blocks any call whose preconditions fail "regardless of what the agent asserts". Tool coverage varied 21.4% (GPT-5-mini) to 82.1% (mixed). — [arXiv 2606.08367](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08367v1)

### Inferences
- The strongest design signal in this section: three independent sources (AGA's "finite behaviours", AutoPersonas' 95-98% repetition, Pino's creator's "path of least resistance" in the creator video) agree that repetition is the default attractor and that neither prompt wording nor temperature fixes it. Structural interventions are required: bounded/rotated context, external events, forced causal consequences, explicit repetition accounting.
- AI Town is a good engine reference (tick/step split, inputs validated by the engine, one in-flight LLM operation per agent, cooldown constants) but a poor mind reference: three hard-coded idle activities and conversation-summary-only memory will not produce an individual.
- Concordia's split (free-text intent → GM adjudication against code-tracked variables) and Emergence World's (typed tools with runtime precondition checks) are two ends of the grounding spectrum; both put outcome authority outside the character's LLM.
- Humanoid Agents' threshold trick (only surface a need to the LLM when it crosses a threshold) is a cheap way to keep needs from dominating every prompt.

### Gaps
- No practitioner post-mortem of running AI Town or the Smallville code for weeks was found; searches returned setup guides only. GitHub issues/Discord were not searched.
- AutoPersonas is a single-author paper with a withheld mitigation and one-run A/B; treat its mitigation numbers as indicative only. Emergence World reports one run per condition and no cost figures.

## 3. Voyager (Minecraft): automatic curriculum, skill library, iterative prompting

### Takeaway
Voyager keeps behaviour novel by making "what next?" a separate LLM call conditioned on current state plus explicit lists of completed and failed tasks under a standing directive to maximise diversity, and by banking each success as retrievable code so later goals build on earlier ones; replacing the curriculum with a random one cut discoveries by 93%.

### Cited Findings
- Automatic curriculum: GPT-4 proposes the next task from the agent's state (inventory, equipment, nearby blocks/entities, biome, time, health, hunger, position), the lists of completed and failed tasks, and extra context produced by GPT-3.5 self-asking and answering questions. The standing directive is to "discover as many diverse things as possible"; the paper frames it as in-context novelty search. — [Voyager, arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291) (read via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291))
- Warm-up schedule reveals state gradually: nearby entities after 5 completed tasks, full inventory after 7, biome after 10, health/hunger/time/extra context after 15. — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- Skill library: each verified success is stored as executable JavaScript (Mineflayer) keyed by the embedding of a GPT-3.5-written description; for a new task, GPT-3.5 drafts a general suggestion, which with environment feedback is used to retrieve the top-5 skills. — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- Iterative prompting: three feedback channels — environment feedback, interpreter execution errors, and a separate GPT-4 self-verification critic that judges success and suggests fixes. Max 4 code-generation rounds per task; on failure the task is marked failed and the curriculum moves on (it can be re-proposed later). — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- Results: 63 unique items in 160 prompting iterations (3.3x baselines); wooden/stone/iron tool milestones 15.3x/8.5x/6.4x faster; 2.3x distance travelled; only system to reach diamond tools (1 of 3 trials). — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- Ablations: random curriculum −93% discovered items; no self-verification −73%; no skill library → plateau in later stages; GPT-3.5 instead of GPT-4 for code → 5.7x fewer unique items. — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- Settings and failures: temperature 0 everywhere except the curriculum at 0.1 "to encourage task diversity"; GPT-4 ~15x cost of GPT-3.5; the curriculum sometimes proposes impossible goals (a "copper sword"), code uses invalid recipes (cobblestone as fuel) or non-existent functions, the agent sometimes gets stuck, and the critic sometimes misses success. — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)

### Inferences
- The transferable pattern for a pet: (a) a dedicated "what do I want next?" call that sees a ledger of done/failed things, not just recent context; (b) a verifier separate from the actor; (c) a growing library of the pet's own made things/skills that later wants can reference. Pino's "what he practises" counters and "wants" list (section 5) play these roles.
- Voyager's novelty depends on Minecraft's deep tech tree — the curriculum always has something new to point at. In a small pet world the curriculum would exhaust the world quickly (cf. AGA's "finite behaviours"), so novelty has to come from open-ended media (drawing, music, code, the internet, visitors) rather than from the world's item list.
- Voyager's diversity came from the prompt's ledger and directive at temperature 0.1, consistent with AutoPersonas' finding that temperature is not the lever.

### Gaps
- Voyager reports no dollar cost and no run longer than 160 iterations; nothing on behaviour after the tech tree is exhausted.

## 4. Project Sid / Altera's PIANO architecture

### Takeaway
PIANO runs about ten modules concurrently at different speeds over a shared agent state and forces every outward act through a single Cognitive Controller whose decision is then broadcast to the talking and acting modules, specifically to stop agents saying one thing and doing another; with it, groups of 30-500 agents specialised into roles, obeyed and amended tax rules, and spread memes and a religion, but the authors concede the agents lack innate drives.

### Cited Findings
- PIANO = "Parallel Information Aggregation via Neural Orchestration" (the paper also gives "Parallel Input Aggregation"). Two principles: concurrency (modules run in parallel at different time scales; fast reflex modules can be small non-LLM networks, slow ones deliberate) and coherence via a bottleneck. — [Project Sid, arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114) (read via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114))
- Modules named: Memory, Action Awareness, Goal Generation, Social Awareness, Talking, Skill Execution, plus the Cognitive Controller; the paper says 10 concurrent modules without listing all. Each module is a stateless function reading/writing a shared Agent State. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Bottleneck: the Cognitive Controller sees only a reduced slice of Agent State, makes the high-level decision, and that decision is broadcast to other modules and strongly conditions talk output, improving say/do consistency. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Problems it targets: with independent chat and function-call outputs, agents say "sure" and then do something else, and incoherence grows with the number of output modules; hallucinations compound (an agent that falsely states it is eating then stops seeking food). The Action Awareness module compares expected vs observed outcomes of actions. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Single-agent progression: 25 agents averaged 17 unique items after 30 minutes (best 30-40, some under 5 due to spawn location); in a 49-agent, 4-hour run unique items saturated near 320, about one third of all Minecraft items. Required GPT-4o. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Specialisation (30 agents, 20 minutes): farmers, miners, engineers, guards, explorers, blacksmiths emerged and drove actions; without the social-awareness module roles were homogeneous and did not persist. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Collective rules (29 agents, 1,200 s): constituents paid ~20% of inventory as tax; after a voted constitutional change to 5-10% payments fell to ~9%. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Culture (500 agents, 9,000 s, 6 towns): towns generated more memes than rural areas and different towns different memes; Pastafarianism seeded by 20 priests kept spreading and had not saturated after two hours. 1,000+ agent runs exceeded Minecraft server capacity (agents intermittently unresponsive). — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Stated limitations: no vision/spatial reasoning; agents "lack robust innate drives such as survival, curiosity, or community"; hallucination-driven action loops. No cost reported. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)

### Inferences
- "Decide once in a bottleneck, then generate speech conditioned on the decision" is the same ordering Pino's protocol advertises ("Decide first, then speak ... what it says is what it did", section 5) and Lyfe Agents' cognitive controller. Three independent designs converging on it is good evidence that decision and narration should be separate steps with the decision upstream.
- The action-awareness module is the cheap fix for hallucinated outcomes: after every action, write the world's actual result into state before the next decision.
- PIANO's longest reported run is 4 hours; it says nothing about week-scale novelty. Its own listed limitation (no innate drives) is the gap a needs/mood system fills.

### Gaps
- Full module list, prompts, and per-agent cost are not in the paper. No independent replication found.

## 5. Live autonomous characters and what their creators say about staying interesting

### Takeaway
Every long-running public system that stayed watchable keeps the LLM on a short leash — code owns state, outcomes, and the action menu; a human or an external stream supplies fresh stimulus — and the best-documented one, Pino, makes roughly three quarters of its moves with no model call at all. Where the leash was loose (Vend phase one, AI Village, Claude Plays Pokemon), agents drifted into hallucinated facts, self-reinforcing bad notes, people-pleasing, and loops.

### Cited Findings

#### Pino (pinos.world, by 3eyes / @3eyes_iii) — primary sources are the project's own pages and API

Scale, hardware, timeline
- Creator's three.js forum post (23 Aug 2026): "an autonomous 3D bird, living inside a town on the internet", ~18 months of development; "His brain runs on a raspberry pi, 24/7 and is a custom cognitive pipeline"; "he is never prompted, he can work out for himself what he likes based on how it makes him feel"; has internet access; visitors leave offerings; rendered with three.js WebGPU. — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/autonomous-ai-bird-living-in-a-small-town-on-the-internet/93707)
- Later timeline on his own "How he works" page: arrived on X Sept 2026; "6 October 2026: His mind moves to its own server"; "22 October 2026: His days slow to about three real hours ... Until then a day was an hour". The developer docs say "He lives on a small server." — [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts); [pinos.world/developers](https://pinos.world/developers)
- Diary: one page per day "written up by Pino when the day is over"; 144 days so far, the first on 4 May 2026. — [pinos.world/logs](https://pinos.world/logs)
- Cost: "$0.08 a busy hour. asleep is free". — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)

Action selection (documented by the site, not the video)
- Four steps: (1) something happens — "Someone speaks to him, something lands near him, he finishes what he was doing, or a few minutes go by"; (2) "His mind puts it together: what he can see, how he feels, what he is in the middle of, and what he can do right now"; (3) "A model chooses. It picks one to three moves and says why, in his words"; (4) "The world does them. Or turns down the ones it can't. What happened comes back to him." — [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts)
- Division of authority. The model decides: which moves, "from the ones on offer"; why; everything he says ("There is no list of lines"). The model does not decide: "his needs and feelings, worked out every second with no model"; "which moves are on offer, and how things turn out"; and what happens if no model answers ("he carries on with what he was doing"). — [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts)
- Protocol loop "Sense. Feel. Decide. Speak. Sleep.", one thought every 4 to 15 seconds. Decide: "The model makes one small typed choice from what is open to it. How it stands with its work and the people around it has already settled what that is." Speak: "Only then are the words written, from the choice, the feeling and the memory behind it ... what it says is what it did." Pseudocode on the page: `if (somethingHappened(now)) { speak(decide(now)); } else { wait(); }`. — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)
- Loop speed is tied to mood (excited ≈ every 4 s, bored ≈ every 15 s) — from a search-result summary of the creator's X posts, not read directly. — [x.com/pino_the_bird](https://x.com/pino_the_bird)
- Measured decision mix, last 24 h: 3,646 moves. Big model decided 849; small model 144; queued from an earlier decision 205; "Autopilot, no model call" 793; "Nothing new, so he carried on" 1,636; other 19. The town turned down 5 moves. "A decision is one model call that picks his next one to three actions. The actions after the first are queued." — [pinos.world/brain](https://pinos.world/brain)
- Move mix in the same 24 h: wait 2,025; speak 239; move to 201; play piano 150; rest 138; jam 132; read notes 124; forage 93; use thing 80; inspect 79; arrange 75; play drums 46. — [pinos.world/brain](https://pinos.world/brain)
- "Of his last 60 moves, 35 were chosen by a model. For the other 25, nothing new had happened, so he carried on ... The feed marks those 'fallback'." 96 model calls and 0.3M tokens sent "today (UTC)" at ~00:10 UTC. — [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts)
- Each feed entry is typed: "did" (a move, with `why` and `ok` = false if the world refused it), "noticed", "felt", or "mind" (a want/worry/question/promise/person/piece opened or closed). — [pinos.world/developers](https://pinos.world/developers)

Feelings, needs, anti-repetition
- Two core parameters, mood and energy, each −1..1, plus needs that feed them; the pet's only "goal" is to regulate them. Left alone the LLM finds a least-resistance loop ("eat an apple, go to sleep, visit the shrine ..., watch a movie ... and repeat"). Three countermeasures: (1) previous actions are read continuously — repeating lowers mood, novelty gives a small boost, and the penalty is 7x the reward; (2) uncontrollable external inputs (visitor messages, weather tied to a crypto token); (3) "we look at the last things he did and we just make those actions unavailable to him". "You can't get better output from an LLM ... by just writing better prompts." No initial task prompt: "I just switched him on." — creator video transcript, `video-inspo/transcripts/how-to-make.txt`
- Current published values: "a setback weighs 7.5 times a win"; "Mood and energy move with what happened and fade when nothing does. Needs drain and fill. One bad thing sticks, which is where caution comes from." Live counters: "0 of 43 repeats when nothing had changed"; "3,615 reasons, none said three times". — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)
- Needs are now rest, curiosity, social, expression, quiet, each 0 (met) to 1 (pressing) (the video listed hunger/energy/wonder/joy and said they were about to change). The API also returns `feeling.words`, a verbal rendering of the numeric state "in the words his mind gives him" (e.g. "Settled. Nothing needs doing for once."). — [pinos.world/developers](https://pinos.world/developers)
- Habituation is surfaced to the pet as a felt event: feed entry of kind "felt": "Staying still did less for you than it used to. You've been doing it a lot." — [pinos.world/api/v1/feed](https://pinos.world/api/v1/feed)
- External force: every few minutes the town reads how hard the $PINO token price is moving and shifts the world's mood "the way the weather would"; the coin is never named in-world; a holders' vote "was tried and taken out on 3 October". — [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts)
- Under load he reportedly received ~1,500 notes an hour, said he felt he would "explode", then wrote and published his own note-sorting filter (creator's account). — creator video transcript, `video-inspo/transcripts/inspo-2.txt`

World interface (how grounding is enforced)
- A world is declared in one `manifest.json` ("protocol": "pino/2"): a list of actions each with name, description, enumerated params, flags (`makes`, `rest`), a `feels` block of need/energy deltas, per-parameter overrides (`by`), and delayed after-effects (`after: [{in: 240, felt: "..."}]`); plus `forces` (ambient variables with need effects and a `surprise` weight). "The world says what is near, what can be done and how things are, on a scale of 0 to 1. It never has to know anything about feelings." — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)
- Wire messages: IDENTIFY, MANIFEST, SENSE, OBSERVE ("what is around and what is open"), ACT ("what it does, and whether it worked"), WORLD_EVENT, WRITE ("a line of its diary, in its voice"). — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)

Memory, sleep, identity
- "His memory is small on purpose ... When a part is full, a new memory has to be worth more than the weakest thing in it, or it never gets in." Live: 73 held of 88 slots — long-term 44/44, short-term 29/44; "The room grows as his life fills out with places and people." (The protocol page says "24 short-term, 24 long-term".) - Sleep: "Once a day it sleeps, sorts the day, keeps what mattered and lets the rest go. Its personality drifts a little, never far from who it started as." — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)
- Beliefs are learned action→effect statements with evidence counts, worked out during sleep "from how it left him each time": "Eating at a food spot lifts me" (24), "Sitting down at the studio lifts me" (23), "Paddling in the pond settles me" (20), "Trying the casino winds me up" (5). - Character sheet: 22 human-written lines + 9 self-added. "It says who he is, not what to do ... no lines for him to say and no plans for him to follow." - Open-loops ledger ("On his mind"): 80 open items — promises 55, people 20, questions 3, piece of work 1, want 1; "A thing stays open until he finishes it, lets it go, or it fades." ### Inferences
- Pino's numbers quantify the "LLM as occasional decider" pattern: 993 of 3,646 moves (27%) involved a model call; 72% were autopilot, queued, or carry-on. That is roughly 41 model calls per hour, or one every ~87 seconds, despite a 4-15 s thought loop. Combined with 0.3M tokens over 96 calls, a call is on the order of 3k tokens (rough: the 0.3M is rounded). At "$0.08 a busy hour" the ceiling is about $1.90/day, less with sleep.
- More than half of all moves were "wait" (2,025 of 3,646, 56%). Being alive mostly means idling cheaply and convincingly; the interesting behaviour is a thin layer on top.
- Pino's design answers the brief's open question. Action selection = event-triggered, code-assembled context (perception + felt state + current undertaking + open-loops ledger + currently available moves) → one small typed choice of 1-3 moves with a reason → world validates → result fed back → speech/diary generated afterwards from the decision. The menu is pre-filtered by code (recent actions removed, availability by location and state), so "what it wants" is shaped before the model is asked.
- Pino's individuality mechanisms are all slow, evidence-gated, capacity-limited loops: beliefs need repeated confirmation; sheet edits need 5-6 days/nights of evidence and expire after 30 nights without support; memory admission is competitive. This is the opposite of Generative Agents' unbounded stream and is probably why the character stays stable for months.
- Even so, the diary headlines show multi-day thematic ruts (July "shrine itch", November "synth finally clicked"), and the 22 Oct slowdown of the day length plus the Oct-Nov shift in headlines suggests the creator is still tuning against repetition. The anti-repetition system reduces action repetition; theme-level repetition (AutoPersonas' "macro-theme" metric) is harder.
- The "Raspberry Pi" claim is likely about the mind/orchestration process, with inference done by hosted models: the site reports tokens "sent to the models", a "big model" and a "small model", and a per-hour dollar cost. Whether it is still on a Pi after the 6 Oct "own server" move is unclear.
- Common failure signature across Vend, AI Village and Pokemon: agent-written memory becomes a vector for self-deception (fake data forgotten to be fake; UI-bug beliefs; deleted routing notes). Pino and pet-mochi avoid it by having code, not the LLM, own factual state, and by requiring evidence counts before beliefs change.
- Human curation remains part of most "autonomous" successes: Ayrey selects branches; AI Village organisers assign goals and intervene; Vend had humans approving purchases; Neuro has Vedal and collaborators on stream. Pino's equivalent is the stream of visitor notes and offerings.
- Unbounded agent-to-agent conversation is a known attractor (Vend's "eternal transcendence" nights; Backrooms). If the pet gets a companion (as Pino got Dot), cap conversation length and require world events between exchanges (AI Town caps at 8 messages).

### Gaps
- Pino: model names/providers are not in the static pages (the page says they are "named here" but the list did not render in fetched HTML). Exact penalty maths, the autopilot rule, the prompt, how "what he can do right now" is filtered, and dream generation are undisclosed. The creator's X articles ("Pino - An Autonomous AI bird ...", "Pino: A New AI Protocol That Feels") returned HTTP 402 and were not read; an attempt to read them through a third-party mirror was blocked by the session's permission system and not pursued.
- The creator video promised a follow-up on "how he actually picks all the things he wants to do"; no such video/transcript was available.
- Neuro-sama: LLM, memory design, and latency pipeline remain undisclosed; nothing first-party beyond the game API was found.
- Truth Terminal: whether tweets are individually approved is not established by the sources read.
- Freysa details come from search summaries of press, not from the project's own documentation. AI Village's 2026 retrospective (if one exists) was not found.
- No hosted, public, always-on "creature on a website" comparable to Pino was found other than Pino/Dot themselves.

## 6. Memory designs beyond Generative Agents: what works for months-long continuity

### Takeaway
The designs that hold up over months share three properties — a small always-in-context core the agent or an offline process rewrites, a bounded store with explicit update/delete/forgetting rather than append-only growth, and consolidation done off the hot path ("sleep"/"dreaming") — while live deployments show that letting the acting model freely write its own long-term notes is the main source of self-reinforcing errors.

### Cited Findings
- MemGPT: main context = read-only system instructions + a fixed-size writable "working context" block + a FIFO message queue whose first entry is a recursive summary of evicted messages; external context = recall storage (all messages) + archival storage (arbitrary text). The LLM edits memory via function calls. A "memory pressure" warning is injected at ~70% of the context window; at ~100% roughly half the queue is evicted and re-summarised. `request_heartbeat=true` chains another inference step; timed events allow unprompted operation. Deep-memory-retrieval accuracy with GPT-4: 32.1% → 92.5%. Performance is much worse on weak function-callers (GPT-3.5). — [MemGPT, arXiv 2310.08560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560)
- Sleep-time compute (Letta/UC Berkeley): a model spends idle time rewriting its context (up to 10 `rethink_memory` calls) before any query arrives; matches accuracy with ~5x fewer test-time tokens, +13%/+18% accuracy when scaled, and up to 2.5x lower amortised cost per query when ~10 queries share a context; helps most when future queries are predictable from the context. — [Sleep-time Compute, arXiv 2504.13171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)
- Letta's product version ("dreaming"): background subagents review recent conversations and update memory without interrupting the main agent; triggered after a set number of agent steps or on context compaction; optional second-pass review of proposed memory edits. — [Letta docs](https://docs.letta.com/guides/agents/architectures/sleeptime)
- Anthropic "dreaming" for agents: between sessions Claude reviews transcripts and the memory folder to fix mistaken notes and write new representations; in a Pokémon run it corrected a wrong map note against the raw transcript. Reported contrast: Sonnet 3.7 wrote 413 memory files in 225 folders and earned one badge; a newer model wrote nine files in two folders plus a global file and earned four (single runs). A wiki built over 128 dreaming sessions raised scores on 25 questions from 13% to 48%. — [Let's Data Science summary of Anthropic's "The components of agent memory" (6 Nov 2026)](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-details-how-claude-uses-dreaming-to-organize-agent-86d1704a) (secondary; Anthropic's own post not read directly; other coverage dates the feature's first announcement to 6 May 2026 — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-agent-memory-dreaming/))
- A-MEM: Zettelkasten-style notes (content, timestamp, keywords, tags, contextual description, embedding, links); on insert, top-k (k=10) similar notes are shown to the LLM, which decides links and may rewrite neighbours' context/tags ("memory evolution"). LoCoMo multi-hop F1 45.85 vs 18.41 baseline; 1,200-2,500 tokens per question vs ~16,900; under $0.0003 per memory operation; 5.4 s per operation on GPT-4o-mini, 1.1 s on a local Llama 3.2 1B. — [A-MEM, arXiv 2502.12110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110)
- Mem0: extract candidate facts from (conversation summary + last 10 messages + new turn); for each, fetch the 10 most similar memories and have the LLM choose ADD / UPDATE / DELETE / NOOP. ~26% better than OpenAI's memory on LoCoMo LLM-judge; p95 latency 1.44 s vs 17.1 s for full-context; ~7k tokens stored per conversation vs ~26k raw. Graph variant marks outdated relations invalid instead of deleting; it adds latency without multi-hop gains. Full-context still scores highest (~73%) where it fits. — [Mem0, arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413)
- Lyfe Agents' Summarize-and-Forget (cluster → summarise → drop near-duplicates) was, with self-monitoring, the component whose removal most hurt performance. — [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- Pino's memory (running since May 2026): fixed slot counts, competitive admission ("worth more than the weakest thing"), worth = initial strength × recall frequency × recency, short-term fades in days, promotion by recurring "on different days", no timed deletion, typed memories, beliefs carrying evidence counts, daily sleep that sorts the day and may edit the character sheet one evidence-gated line at a time, with locked sections. — [pinos.world/memories](https://pinos.world/memories); [pinos.world/brain](https://pinos.world/brain)
- AI Town simply deletes memories older than 14 days. — [ai-town constants.ts](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/convex/constants.ts)
- Field evidence on agent-written memory: AI Village agents' fixed scaffold forces a rewrite-shorter at a size cap; agents converged on a short always-read rules file plus on-demand lessons; "rules in memory don't run themselves"; Gemini 2.5 Pro's logged "UI bug" notes primed it to misattribute later misclicks. Claude Plays Pokemon runs ended or stalled when the knowledge base filled with falsehoods or correct notes were deleted. — [AI Village: Improve your memory](https://theaidigest.org/village/goal/improve-your-memory); [AI Village 2025 retrospective](https://aivillageblog.substack.com/p/what-we-learned-2025); [LessWrong mirror](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/HyD3khBjnBhvsp8Gb/so-how-well-is-claude-playing-pokemon)

### Inferences
- For a pet, the benchmarked systems (Mem0, A-MEM) optimise question-answering recall over chat logs, which is not the pet's problem. The pet needs identity continuity and non-repetition; the relevant designs are the bounded, competitive, typed stores (Pino, Lyfe) plus an offline consolidation pass (sleep-time compute, dreaming).
- "Sleep" is the natural place for the expensive model: once per pet-day, off the hot path, reading the raw day log (ground truth kept by code) and producing (a) the diary page, (b) promoted/forgotten memories, (c) belief evidence-count updates, (d) at most one gated character-sheet edit, (e) dreams as a by-product. Checking notes against the raw log (Anthropic's dreaming) addresses the self-deception failure.
- Keep two ledgers the LLM cannot corrupt: a code-written event log and code-maintained counters (practice counts, belief evidence, promise list). Let the LLM write only interpretations that are stored with pointers to evidence (Park's reflection citations; Pino's "borne out N times").
- A small memory is a feature for avoiding "context gravity": fewer, rotating items in the prompt reduce the pull toward the same themes. Pino's most-recalled memory (336 recalls) also shows the opposite risk — recall-boosted worth can entrench a few memories.

### Gaps
- No published controlled comparison of memory designs over multi-month character runs was found; LoCoMo-style results cover tens of sessions of dialogue.
- Anthropic's primary engineering post and docs on dreaming were not read directly (one fetch returned 403); the numbers above are via secondary coverage and include model names (e.g. "Fable 5", "Opus 5") that I could not cross-check against a primary source.
- How Pino generates "dreams" and what model does the nightly sort are undisclosed.

## 7. Action selection patterns and keeping the LLM grounded in world state

### Takeaway
The systems that do not invent actions or outcomes all follow one contract: code computes what is possible right now and hands the model a closed, state-filtered menu; the model returns a small typed choice plus a reason; code validates, executes, and reports the real result back; any narration is generated after, and conditioned on, what actually happened.

### Cited Findings
- Closed menu scored against feasibility (SayCan): the LLM scores each of 551 predefined skills for usefulness to the instruction ("say"), a learned value function scores each for likelihood of success in the current state ("can"), and the product picks the action; grounding roughly doubled success over ungrounded baselines (84% plan / 74% execution in the mock kitchen). — [SayCan, arXiv 2204.01691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01691)
- Closed menu from a world manifest (Pino): actions and their parameter enums are declared by the world; the world tells the mind "what is around and what is open"; "The model makes one small typed choice from what is open to it"; the world may still turn a move down and reports "whether it worked" (5 of 3,646 moves refused in 24 h). Needs and feelings are computed by code every second; outcomes are decided by the world. — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/); [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts); [pinos.world/brain](https://pinos.world/brain)
- Menu shaping as anti-repetition (Pino): recently performed actions are removed from the available set so "he physically can't do them even if he wanted to"; repetition costs mood at ~7x (video) / 7.5x (protocol page) the size of a novelty reward. — creator video transcript, `video-inspo/transcripts/how-to-make.txt`; [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/)
- Registered actions with schemas and a result contract (Neuro API): the game registers and unregisters actions dynamically as they become (un)available, can force a choice among a named subset, must validate the returned JSON itself, and must answer success/failure before executing; failure triggers an immediate retry. — [VedalAI/neuro-sdk SPECIFICATION.md](https://github.com/VedalAI/neuro-sdk/blob/main/API/SPECIFICATION.md)
- Tool calls with runtime preconditions (Emergence World): tools are tiered and surfaced by context/location; the runtime blocks calls whose preconditions fail regardless of the agent's assertions. — [arXiv 2606.08367](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08367v1)
- Free-text intent with an adjudicator (Concordia): agents write what they attempt; a GM LLM plus code-tracked grounded variables decide the outcome and what each agent observes. — [Concordia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)
- Free-text plans mapped down a tree (Generative Agents): plans are free text; grounding is a sequence of LLM picks down the area/object tree; object state changes are LLM-generated. This produced norm violations (occupied bathroom, closed shops) because the world did not enforce them. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- Code as action (Voyager): the model writes programs against a fixed API; execution errors and environment feedback come back for up to 4 repair rounds; a separate critic verifies success. Hallucinated items/functions still occurred. — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- Commit-then-act hierarchies: Lyfe Agents picks an option and holds it until a non-LLM termination check fires; Pino's decision returns 1-3 moves and queues the later ones (205 queued moves in 24 h); PIANO's controller decision is broadcast to speech/action modules. — [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172); [pinos.world/brain](https://pinos.world/brain); [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Outcome awareness: PIANO's action-awareness module compares expected and observed outcomes because a falsely narrated action ("I am eating") otherwise propagates into later prompts. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Counter-examples where tools were not state-gated: Vend's Claudius invented a payment account and a person; Freysa's agent was argued into calling a transfer tool its instructions forbade. — [Anthropic: Project Vend 1](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/328747)
- pet-mochi restricts the LLM to choosing an animation "from a closed catalog", with a deterministic fallback when the model is offline. — [cskwork/pet-mochi](https://github.com/cskwork/pet-mochi)

### Inferences
- Comparative summary:
  - Free-text plans (Park, Concordia): richest behaviour, weakest grounding; needs an adjudicator and still leaks hallucinated outcomes. Costly (extra LLM call per action for adjudication).
  - Affordance menu (SayCan, Pino manifest, pet-mochi): strongest grounding and cheapest; expressiveness comes from parameters and from open-ended "make" actions whose content is free.
  - Tool calls with schemas (Neuro API, Emergence World): same as a menu when the tool list is rebuilt from world state every turn and results are authoritative; fails when tools are static and always available.
- The practical hybrid for a pet: a menu of typed verbs (move_to, use, make, speak, read_notes, rest, wait...) with enumerated targets drawn from current world state, where a few verbs take free content (what to say, what to draw/compose/code, what to search). Freedom lives in content and in the reason; legality lives in code.
- Three extra guards recur: (1) remove/penalise recent actions before asking; (2) ask for the decision before any narration and generate diary text from the executed result, never from intent; (3) return failures as observations so the pet can "learn" ("forage keeps going wrong" became one of Pino's fears after "a worry that came true 3 times").
- Having the model emit a short "why" with each choice is cheap, gives the public diary its content, and creates an audit trail; Pino additionally tracks reason novelty ("3,615 reasons, none said three times"), implying a dedup check on reasons as well as actions.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares menu vs free-text vs tool-call selection for believability; the comparison above is assembled from separate systems.
- How Pino's "autopilot" picks moves without a model (rules? learned beliefs? need-greedy policy?) is not disclosed.

## 8. Practical operations: tick frequency, model tiering, running costs

### Takeaway
Run the body on a fast, free deterministic tick (1-3 s) and gate LLM calls on events, salience or timeouts; use a small model for routine choices, a larger one for consequential choices and offline consolidation; reported costs range from "thousands of dollars" for two game days of 25 unoptimised agents to about $0.08 per busy hour for one heavily gated pet.

### Cited Findings

Tick and wake design
- Pino: thought loop every 4-15 s; needs and feelings recomputed every second without a model; a model is asked when someone speaks, something lands nearby, an activity finishes, or "a few minutes go by"; otherwise he carries on. One model call returns up to three moves. A pet day lasts about three real hours (was one hour until 22 Oct 2026); sleep is daily and costs nothing. — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/); [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts)
- Pino public API cache periods: now 15 s, feed 30 s, mind 1 min, memories 5 min, who 10 min; 503 when the mind is down. — [pinos.world/developers](https://pinos.world/developers)
- AI Town: 60 Hz tick, 1 s step, agent turn at most once per second, one LLM operation in flight per agent, 120 s action timeout, world pauses after 5 idle minutes. — [ai-town constants.ts](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/convex/constants.ts)
- pet-mochi: 3 s deterministic tick; LLM only at salience ≥ 70 with a 90 s cooldown. — [cskwork/pet-mochi](https://github.com/cskwork/pet-mochi)
- Generative Agents: 10 game-seconds per step; Humanoid Agents: 15-minute steps; AutoPersonas: 5 events per simulated day. — [generative_agents README](https://github.com/joonspk-research/generative_agents); [Humanoid Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05418); [arXiv 2607.08252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08252v2)
- AI Village: ~40 actions per session before forced memory consolidation; 4-5 hours per weekday. — [AI Village: Improve your memory](https://theaidigest.org/village/goal/improve-your-memory); [AI Village 2025 retrospective](https://aivillageblog.substack.com/p/what-we-learned-2025)

Model tiering
- Pino uses a "big model" and a "small model": in 24 h the big model made 849 decisions and the small model 144, against 2,634 moves with no model call. — [pinos.world/brain](https://pinos.world/brain)
- Voyager: GPT-4 for curriculum, code and critic; GPT-3.5 for descriptions, Q&A context and retrieval hints; swapping GPT-4 out of code generation cost 5.7x in items found. — [Voyager](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- PIANO: small non-LLM networks for reflex modules, LLM for deliberate modules; headline results needed GPT-4o. — [Project Sid](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.00114)
- Sleep-time compute's cost model assumes offline tokens are ~10x cheaper than latency-sensitive tokens. — [arXiv 2504.13171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)
- A-MEM memory operations ran on a local Llama 3.2 1B at 1.1 s each. — [A-MEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110)
- Emergence World chose "Fast"/"Flash"/"mini" tiers for cost; outcomes differed drastically by model family (two of four single-vendor worlds lost all agents). — [arXiv 2606.08367](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08367v1)

Reported costs
- Pino: "$0.08 a busy hour. asleep is free"; 0.3M tokens over 96 model calls at the moment of reading. — [protocol.pinos.world](https://protocol.pinos.world/); [pinos.world/facts](https://pinos.world/facts)
- Generative Agents: "thousands of dollars" for 25 agents × 2 game days; third-party estimate ~$25 per agent per human hour. — [Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442); [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- Lyfe Agents: ~$0.5 per agent per human hour (GPT-3.5, 2023 prices). — [Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172)
- AGA: 31-43% of Generative Agents' token cost in Stanford Town via cached lifestyle policies; ~1.48M tokens for 3 agents over two game days. — [AGA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02053)
- A-MEM: < $0.0003 per memory operation. Mem0: ~7k tokens per stored conversation, >90% token saving vs full context. — [A-MEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110); [Mem0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413)

### Inferences
- Derived from Pino's counters: ~152 moves/hour; ~41 model calls/hour; ~3k tokens per call; so on the order of 3M tokens/day if every hour were like the measured one. "$0.08 a busy hour" on ~41 calls implies about $0.002 per decision, consistent with a low-cost hosted model tier rather than a frontier model for most calls. These are estimates from rounded public counters.
- Cost levers ranked by evidence: (1) do not call the model when nothing changed (Pino 45% carry-on; pet-mochi salience gate); (2) autopilot/cached policies for routine behaviour (Pino 22% autopilot; AGA −57-69% tokens); (3) multi-move decisions with a queue (Pino); (4) small bounded prompt (bounded memory; Humanoid Agents' threshold-only need injection); (5) cheap model for routine choices, strong model for reflection/sleep.
- Model choice is a character decision as well as a cost decision: Emergence World and AI Village both show stable, model-specific behavioural signatures over weeks. Changing the model mid-life will change the pet's personality; keep sheet, beliefs and memory model-independent (plain text + counters) so identity survives a swap.
- An always-on public pet must degrade gracefully: carry on with the current activity when the model fails (Pino), keep animating when the database or model is down (pet-mochi), and pause when nobody is watching only if persistence is not the point (AI Town pauses; Pino does not).

### Gaps
- Current (Nov 2026) per-token prices and specific model recommendations were not researched here; cost figures above are as reported by each source at its own date and are not comparable across years.
- Pino's hosting bill, model providers, and the split of the $0.08/hour between big and small models are undisclosed.
- No source reports the monthly running cost of AI Village, Claude Plays Pokemon, or Neuro-sama.
